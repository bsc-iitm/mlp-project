Evaluation & Passing Criteria

To pass this project course, you must meet all four criteria:

  1. Registration: The registration must be completed successfully by Nov 1, 2026.
  2. Cutoff Score: The submission from the registered notebook must cross the score cutoff in the Kaggle Leaderboard before the deadline.
  3. Level 1 Viva: Must be cleared in the designated batch. Students who do not clear in the first attempt will be given a chance to reattempt. Failing the reattempt requires repeating the project next term.
  4. Level 2 Viva: Must be cleared in the designated batch by scoring at least 25/50 marks. Failing the Level 2 viva requires repeating the project next term. There will be no reattempt for Level 2 viva.

Total Score Calculation

The formula for computing the Total Score (out of 100) is: Final marks = NB + M + L + S + V

Where:

  • NB: Score of your notebook out of 10 marks (assigned by the viva examiner).

  • M: 5 marks awarded for successfully completing all milestones.

  • L: Marks based on Kaggle Leaderboard performance, scaled from 0 to 30.

    \[L = 30 \times \frac{\text{Your best score} - \text{cutoff score}}{\text{Top leaderboard score} - \text{cutoff score}}\]

  • S: Marks based on the number of submissions, scaled from 0 to 5.

    \[ S = \begin{cases} 5, & \text{if public score is in top 20\% of leaderboard} \\ 0, & \text{if } N < 10 \\ 5 \times \frac{N - 10}{N_{\max} - 10}, & \text{if } N \ge 10 \end{cases} \]

    (Where \(N\) is the number of submissions made, and \(N_{\max}\) is the maximum number of submissions made as per the leaderboard).

  • V: Marks in Viva Voce, out of 50 marks.

Competition & Rules

General Rules

  1. Participate individually. This is not a group project.
  2. No external data can be used to train your model.
  3. Do not use any accelerators available on the Kaggle platform (use a CPU-only environment).
  4. Submitting at least 2 out of 5 milestones is mandatory to complete the project.
  5. You can make a maximum of 5 submissions every day.
  6. Submissions made by explicitly uploading .csv, .zip, or any other file will not be counted. Only submissions made from the FinalNotebook will be considered.
WarningDisqualification & Penalties

Your project will be discarded and you will fail the course if:

  1. You submit a different Kaggle notebook link in the Registration Form than the one you use to make submissions.
  2. The final Python notebook (FinalNotebook) is not shared with the competition host.
  3. You are found plagiarizing from the internet, a fellow student, or any external sources.
  4. You use unauthorized machine learning libraries.
  5. Your notebook is made public (it must remain private).

Viva Examinations

Level 1 Viva

The Level-1 Viva is designed to ensure that each participant in the Kaggle Project has put in a significant amount of effort in their MLP Project. This ensures that only participants who have shown dedication and effort in their Project work are eligible for the Level-2 Viva.

Important

Completing all items on the checklist is mandatory.

Level 1 Viva Checklist

The following components will be tested in the Level 1 viva:

  1. Problem statement and dataset
    • The problem statement is understood correctly.
    • The dataset is appropriately loaded.
  2. End-to-end analytical reasoning
    • The focus throughout the notebook from EDA → Preprocessing → Feature Engineering → Model Building → Error / Performance Analysis is to highlight the insights and explain the influence of each step on the subsequent approach.
  3. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
    • Objective: Understand key statistics and relationships in the dataset.
    • Includes univariate and bivariate feature analysis on the relevant features.
    • Use appropriate visualizations that are clear, readable, and relevant.
  4. Systematic preprocessing of the data
    • Detection and appropriate handling of missing values.
    • Need for appropriate scaling of numerical features.
    • Need for appropriate encoding of categorical features.
    • All data transformations (data cleaning, scaling, encoding, etc.) should be implemented using reusable pipelines or custom functions and consistently applied to both training and test datasets to avoid data leakage, ensure reproducibility, and reduce redundant code.
  5. Feature Engineering / Extraction
    • Creation of new features / transformation of existing features followed by analysis of their importance.
  6. Model experimentation and hyperparameter tuning
    • Experiment with multiple models from different families followed by hyperparameter tuning to optimize performance.
  7. Model comparison
    • Compare the performance of at least three different models on training / validation data using appropriate evaluation metrics.
    • Highlight the advantages and disadvantages of each model.
  8. Notebook quality
    • The notebook should be clean, well-structured, appropriately commented, and contain the relevant output.
  9. Best-performing model
    • The notebook submission should contain the best-performing model.
Note

The Level-1 viva serves as a check to see if minimum requirements are met before attempting Level-2. The final scores will depend only on the Level-2 performance.

Repeat L1 Viva

If a student does not meet the minimum requirements in the Level-1 viva, they will get another chance to clear the Level-1 viva provided it is attempted in Batch 1.

Level 2 Viva Preparation

  1. Explain your code: Be prepared to provide an in-depth explanation of your code, including the reasoning behind your choices and the logic implemented as requested by the examiner.
  2. Conceptual Knowledge: Examiners may ask questions from any DS diploma course you’ve taken (MLF, MLT, statistics, etc.), not just the specific project domain.
  3. Internal Mechanics: Using an API is not enough. You must understand the 5 components of any used model: Data, Model, Loss function, Optimization, and Evaluation.
  4. Live Coding: You may be asked to write code from scratch or modify your existing code during the exam.
